## Competitor evaluations

In the market space, there are some various competitors, per say. They’re not the same thing as Supra Human, but they have apps in the mobile stores that I can review how they describe their products and use that to get an idea of how others approach the listings.

### Future

“Personal training, reimagined”

They center theirs on the dedicated coach and a plan that adapts around goals, fitness level, and lifestyle. The Google Play listing is even more relational: “Not just an app, not just a personal trainer.”

### Trainwell

“You deserve more than just a workout plan”

They also focus on the human trainer as the first major feature.

### Caliber

This seems like the strongest showing in the ecosystem: strength training, cardio, nutrition, habits, community, and premium 1:1 coaching. It makes the app feel like a system rather than a workout tracker.

### Ladders

Simpler and more product-led talks about structured coach-built plans, no guesswork, easy execution, visible progress, and team/community support. Its listing reads very clearly.

### Noom

This one’s a good benchmark for AI nutrition language because it doesn’t make AI the star. It says basically: log meals with AI, understand habits, get coaching and community. AI is friction reduction, not the brand promise.

### WHOOP

Might be the closest premium-performance framing: turns a lot of health data into a “daily action.” That’s helpful because Supra Human leans into how raw tracking isn’t the value; interpretation and action are.
